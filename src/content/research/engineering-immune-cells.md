Cell therapy today is made one patient at a time. Immune cells are drawn from a
donor, engineered in a facility, and shipped back — weeks of work, at great
expense, and it fails outright when a patient is too sick to donate usable cells.

We work on making immune cells a manufactured product instead.

## A renewable source

Human pluripotent stem cells divide indefinitely, can be edited once and banked,
and — given the right sequence of signals — can be pushed down the blood lineage
into functional effector cells. We develop **chemically defined** protocols that
run from hemogenic endothelium to definitive hematopoietic progenitors and on to
neutrophils, natural killer cells and macrophages. Every step is a specified
molecule at a specified time, which is what makes a protocol transferable to
another lab and, eventually, to a manufacturing floor.

## Beyond T cells

A chimeric antigen receptor fuses a fragment that recognises a tumour marker to
the signalling machinery that tells an immune cell to kill. CAR-T cells have
transformed the treatment of blood cancers; solid tumours have been far more
stubborn. So we build CARs into cells that T cells cannot replace — neutrophils,
which cross the blood–brain barrier; macrophages, which eat what they kill and
present the debris to the rest of the immune system; NK cells, which do not cause
graft-versus-host disease and so can come from a stranger.

More recently we have been generating CAR-neutrophils **inside the body**,
removing the manufacture-and-infuse step altogether.

## Cells that keep working

Engineered cells fade. We build circuits that let a cell sense a hallmark of the
tumour microenvironment and respond by arming itself further rather than
exhausting, and adapter systems that decouple the receptor from its target — one
cell product, redirected by a separately dosed molecule, against many antigens.

The same lever applies outside cancer. A macrophage's phenotype determines
whether it degrades tissue or rebuilds it, and that switch is a therapeutic
target in its own right.
