Cell therapy today is made one patient at a time. Immune cells are drawn from a
donor, engineered in a facility, and shipped back — a process that takes weeks,
costs a great deal, and fails outright when a patient is too sick to donate
usable cells.

Human pluripotent stem cells offer a way out. They divide indefinitely, they can
be edited once and banked, and — given the right sequence of signals — they can
be pushed down the blood lineage into functional immune cells.

We develop **chemically defined differentiation protocols** that take stem cells
through hemogenic endothelium to definitive hematopoietic progenitors, and on to
mature effector cells. No feeder layers, no serum, no undefined components: every
step is a specified molecule at a specified time, which is what makes a protocol
transferable to another lab and, eventually, to a manufacturing floor.

## What we are working on

- Improving the yield and maturity of stem-cell-derived neutrophils, which are
  short-lived and notoriously hard to produce in useful numbers.
- Transcription-factor programming to steer differentiation more directly than
  small molecules alone allow.
- Understanding why stem-cell-derived immune cells behave differently from their
  donor-derived counterparts — and closing that gap.
