Immune cells do not only attack. They also decide whether tissue is repaired or
dismantled — and in chronic disease that decision goes the wrong way. The
inflammation that lets a tumour evade treatment is not so different from the
inflammation that wears out a joint or stops a fracture from knitting.

We study that decision in three tissues at once.

## Tumours

How cancer cells and immune cells find each other under flow, how the
inflammatory microenvironment shields a tumour, and how the mechanics of that
encounter shape metastasis. This line of work goes back to cellular
biomechanics and micro-hemodynamics, and it is what makes the delivery
strategies elsewhere in the group tractable.

## Joints

Osteoarthritis has long been described as wear and tear. It is increasingly
clear that vascular and inflammatory signalling drives it. We work on
**endothelin signalling** in the pathogenesis of post-traumatic and post-COVID
osteoarthritis — showing that blocking endothelin receptors mitigates
SARS-CoV-2-induced joint damage — and on endothelial dysfunction as a driver of
chondrocyte senescence.

## Bone

Bone remodelling is governed by the immune and metabolic state of its
microenvironment. We work on **bone immunometabolism**: how macrophages in
adipose tissue signal to bone through osteopontin, how excess glucocorticoids
suppress bone turnover by reshaping the skeletal microenvironment, and how the
crosstalk between bone and other organs sets the pace of repair.

## The common thread

Three tissues, one mechanism. Understanding it in one place tells us where to
intervene in the others — which is the reason this group is organised as one
group rather than three.
